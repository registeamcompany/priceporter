Federal buyers spent $52 billion through the GSA Schedule last year. The paperwork is where most companies give up.

You've probably already looked at it. SAM.gov registration. eOffer. Pricing disclosures. A proposal package that runs dozens of pages before GSA even starts asking questions — and they will ask questions. Clarification letters, resubmissions, price negotiations with a contracting officer who reviews offers like yours all day.

Do it yourself and you're signing up for months of back-and-forth in a system you'll use exactly once. Hand it to a one-man consultant and you risk something worse: a "paper mill" that files a template proposal, collects the fee, and disappears the day your contract is awarded — right when the real work starts.

And the bar keeps rising. Since 2025, GSA has been actively cancelling Schedule contracts that don't meet sales minimums and tightening compliance reviews. Getting awarded is no longer the finish line. Keeping the contract alive and selling is.
